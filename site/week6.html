Week 6 (optional): PX4 SITL, Gazebo and uXRCE-DDS

Take off, for real.

A ROS 2 action server that takes a PX4 quadcopter off to 5 m, and an action client that asks it to. The drone is PX4 {{STAT:w6_px4}} itself, flying the x500 in Gazebo Harmonic, reached through the Micro XRCE-DDS Agent exactly as the PX4 guides set it up, on an 8 GB M1 in Docker. Then the link between them, DDS-XRCE, rebuilt in pure Python, flies the same takeoff with no ROS 2 at all.

      DDS-XRCE, by hand.

      PX4 does not speak DDS. It asks the agent, in DDS-XRCE, to create a participant, then {{STAT:w6_topics}} topics with their writers and readers, in eProsima's binary representations. week06/xrce.py decodes and encodes all of it from the specification; week06/dissect.py rebuilt this session from a capture of the 5 m flight, and week06/xrce_client.py uses the same code to log in to the agent itself.

          What PX4 created, and what carried data.

          From the whole flight's capture: {{STAT:w6_kbps_out}} kbit/s from PX4 and {{STAT:w6_kbps_in}} kbit/s to it, over {{STAT:w6_created}} objects. Rates are per second of wall clock; the M1 ran Gazebo below real time for part of this flight, so in PX4's own time they are higher (sensor_combined runs at 100 Hz).

          {{STAT:w6_xrce_hold}} m
          python -m week06 xrce fly: the takeoff server's state machine, fed by this repo's XRCE client instead of rclpy. PX4 held it after the 5 m goal, then landed, with no ROS 2 installed in the loop.
          {{STAT:w6_xrce_msgs}} messages
          The pure Python client's session for that flight: log in, {{STAT:w6_xrce_topics}} topics with their readers and writers, then setpoints at 20 Hz and telemetry back.
          {{STAT:w6_px4_msgs}} types
          px4_msgs definitions PX4 bridges by default, parsed by week 5's IDL code and encoded with its CDR, so samples decode to PX4's own field names.

          Seven hops from a goal to a motor.

          What happens between takeoff_client asking for 5 m and the x500's rotors spinning up. Every hop is something that has to be right before the drone leaves the ground.

          1. takeoff_clientsends a ugs_interfaces/action/Takeoff goal: altitude, climb rate, method. It gets feedback at 10 Hz and the result, and can cancel.
          2. takeoff_serveraccepts or rejects the goal (altitude 1 to 120 m, one goal at a time), then runs the state machine at 20 Hz: wait for PX4, preflight, stream setpoints, switch to offboard, arm, climb a trapezoidal profile, settle.
          3. ROS 2 topics/fmu/in/offboard_control_mode, /fmu/in/trajectory_setpoint and /fmu/in/vehicle_command out; /fmu/out/vehicle_status_v1, vehicle_local_position_v1, vehicle_land_detected and vehicle_command_ack in, subscribed best effort or they never match.
          4. Micro XRCE-DDS Agentowns the DDS participant, readers and writers PX4 asked for. To ROS 2 they look like any other node's; to PX4 they are objects in an XRCE session on UDP 8888.
          5. uxrce_dds_clientPX4's module: turns XRCE samples into uORB messages and back. PX4 1.16 and later suffix versioned messages, hence _v1.
          6. commander and the controllersrefuse offboard until setpoints stream, refuse to arm until the preflight checks pass, fall back to hold one second after the setpoints stop, and fly the position setpoints through the multicopter position and attitude controllers.
          7. gz_bridge and Gazebosend motor commands to the simulated x500 and sensor data (IMU, barometer, magnetometer, GPS) back to PX4's estimator, in lockstep.

          The brief warns that this is not straightforward. The first nine are the ones this repo hit, in the order it hit them; the last four are PX4 rules it was built around, each broken on purpose by the tests. Every fix is in the code, and in docs/week06/NOTES.md.

          Read it, run it.

          Everything runs in one image, ugs-px4:jazzy, built from ros2_ws/px4/Dockerfile on top of week 5's: PX4 {{STAT:w6_px4}} built for SITL, Gazebo Harmonic, px4_msgs and the agent. On Ubuntu 24.04 with ROS 2 Jazzy, the PX4 guides' own steps work just as well; sitl.launch.py only needs PX4_DIR.
